Zhuo (Zoe) Li

PhD Student in Psychology (Cognitive Neuroscience), University of Texas at Austin

Memory and Aging Lab

Austin, TX, USA

I study human memory and cognition, combining behavioral, neuroimaging, and statistical methods to understand how the brain supports memory across cultures and aging. Currently, I work with Dr. Audrey Duarte at the Memory and Aging Lab, where I study cultural differences in how the brain processes object-context relationships in episodic memory. During my undergrad, I worked with Dr. Thad Polk at the Computational & Cognitive Neuroscience Lab and Dr. Shinobu Kitayama at the Culture & Cognition Lab.

I’m also interested in applying data analysis and research skills to real-world, industry problems.

Research

Cultural differences in episodic memory. Investigating how the brain processes object-context relationships in episodic memory differently across cultures, at the Memory and Aging Lab (UT Austin). Built Python preprocessing pipelines and applied EEG time-frequency and independent component analyses across 200+ participants.

GABA, neural distinctiveness, and memory in aging. My undergraduate honors thesis, part of the MiND study at the Computational & Cognitive Neuroscience Lab (Michigan): examined how age-related GABA levels relate to neural distinctiveness and memory performance in older adults. Built a MATLAB/R/Bash data pipeline and modeled 50+ variables to identify neural predictors of memory.

GABA/Glx and auditory processing in aging. Explored how GABA and glutamate/glutamine (Glx) concentration in auditory cortex relate to auditory performance in older adults.
